## 1. Scope and method

This document researches how **Western (tropical) sun-sign compatibility** is traditionally derived and presented, so that Vyotish can build a "Zodiac Sign Compatibility" section with its own original content and a defensible scoring model. Everything below is summarised in my own words. No text, scores, or wording from any competitor site has been copied; the Astrotalk study was structure-only (see `astrotalk-comparison.md`).

Method: 10+ sources fetched directly and ~12 more reviewed via search snippets (register in `sources.md`). The four core rule tables (element, modality, ruler, dignities) and the aspect list were each verified against **at least two independent sources**. The Astrotalk compatibility section (hub page, Aries hub page, one pair page) was fetched for structure only.

A framing caveat worth keeping in mind for all copy: mainstream reference sources describe astrology as an interpretive tradition rather than an empirical science [2]. This supports our site policy of "guidance and entertainment" framing rather than predictive claims.

## 2. How sun-sign compatibility is traditionally derived

Sun-sign ("synastry-lite") compatibility compares only the two partners' **Sun signs** — the zodiac sign the Sun occupied at each birth. Full synastry compares whole birth charts, which is out of scope here; sun-sign compatibility is the simplified, sign-pair-only version that compatibility calculators present [1][9][11].

Four independent "lenses" are traditionally combined, and they largely agree with each other because they are all properties of *where each sign sits on the 360-degree wheel*:

1. **Element** (what the sign is made of)
2. **Modality** (how the sign behaves)
3. **Polarity** (its orientation: expressive vs receptive)
4. **Aspect** (the angular distance between the two signs on the wheel)

A fifth, planetary lens (rulers and their dignities) is used by more traditional writers as a modifier. Each lens is detailed below; `pair-matrix.md` encodes all of them into one deterministic score.

### 2.1 The four elements (triplicities)

The twelve signs divide into four groups of three [2][5]:

| Element | Signs | Traditional character |
|---|---|---|
| Fire | Aries, Leo, Sagittarius | self-expressing, energetic, initiating spirit |
| Earth | Taurus, Virgo, Capricorn | practical, material, sensation-oriented |
| Air | Gemini, Libra, Aquarius | mental, social, conceptual |
| Water | Cancer, Scorpio, Pisces | feeling, intuitive, absorbing |

**Pairing rules (VERIFIED across [1], [2], [9] and the element explainers on [14]):**

- **Same element (fire–fire, earth–earth, air–air, water–water):** natural mutual understanding — the easiest elemental pairing. On the wheel this always coincides with a **trine (120°)**, so the element and aspect lenses agree.
- **Complementary elements (fire–air, earth–water):** traditionally supportive. Air "feeds" fire (ideas energise action); water "shapes" earth (feeling gives form to matter). On the wheel these pairs occur only as **sextiles (60°)** or **oppositions (180°)** — easy when sextile, magnetically tense when opposed.
- **Cross elements (fire–earth, air–water):** neither hostile nor natural allies — different tempos (initiative vs stability; intellect vs feeling) that can work with deliberate effort. These occur as squares, semi-sextiles, quincunxes.
- **Contrary elements (fire–water, earth–air):** classically the most difficult — water quenches fire, air stirs up earth (moisture dries). These occur as squares, semi-sextiles, quincunxes.

Note the terminology choice I have documented for our model: I treat fire–earth and air–water as **neutral "cross"** pairs and fire–water and earth–air as **"contrary/hostile"** pairs. This mirrors the classical element-contrariety logic and keeps the scoring granular (see `pair-matrix.md` §3).

### 2.2 The three modalities (quadruplicities)

The twelve signs divide into three groups of four [2][5]:

| Modality | Signs | Traditional character |
|---|---|---|
| Cardinal | Aries, Cancer, Libra, Capricorn | initiates, starts things, pushes |
| Fixed | Taurus, Leo, Scorpio, Aquarius | sustains, resists change, consolidates |
| Mutable | Gemini, Virgo, Sagittarius, Pisces | adapts, flexes, disperses |

**Pairing tendencies (VERIFIED in [1] general modality notes and consistent across studied pair pages):**

- **Cardinal + Cardinal:** both want to lead → control contest unless roles are divided.
- **Fixed + Fixed:** both resist yielding → standoffs; loyalty is high, flexibility low.
- **Mutable + Mutable:** both adapt → flexible but can drift, decide nothing.
- **Cardinal + Fixed:** initiator meets consolidator → productive if the initiator accepts slower pacing.
- **Cardinal + Mutable:** leader meets adapter → workable; risk is one-sided decision-making.
- **Fixed + Mutable:** stabiliser meets flexor → the fixed partner may find the mutable partner unreliable.

Important interaction: signs in the **same modality are always 90° apart (square)** — the square's friction IS largely modality friction. Signs in the same element are always in different modalities, which is why trines feel easy: same material, complementary behaviour. So in our model, modality friction is already priced into the aspect; we expose it in explanations rather than double-counting it in the score.

### 2.3 Polarity (positive/negative, yang/yin)

Signs alternate around the wheel between two polarities [2]:

- **Positive / yang / masculine (energy mode: outward, expressive):** the six "odd-numbered" signs — Aries, Gemini, Leo, Libra, Sagittarius, Aquarius = the **fire and air** signs.
- **Negative / yin / feminine (energy mode: inward, receptive):** the six "even-numbered" signs — Taurus, Cancer, Virgo, Scorpio, Capricorn, Pisces = the **earth and water** signs.

These are energy-mode labels, not gender labels (copy note: avoid gendered framing in our content; "yang/yin" or "expressive/receptive" is the modern phrasing) [23 — low-reliability corroboration; primary source 2].

**Pairing tendencies:** same-polarity pairs share an expressive/receptive rhythm, which the tradition reads as easier rapport; opposite-polarity pairs (which are always **adjacent signs or squares/oppositions**... precisely: adjacent signs always have opposite polarity; same-sign and trine/sextile/opposition pairs always share polarity) must bridge the outward/inward gap. In our model polarity is descriptive content, not a separate score input, because it is 100% implied by the aspect (adjacent = opposite polarity; all other aspects = same polarity).

### 2.4 The aspect-based method (angular distance)

The most systematic traditional method: place both signs on the 360° wheel and read the **angle between them**. Each sign occupies 30°, so the angle is always a multiple of 30°. The seven angles relevant to sign pairs (VERIFIED: aspect list and characterisations from [1]; independent corroboration of the practical reading from [9], whose compatibility grid is built on exactly these seven classes, and from [21]):

| Angular distance | Aspect name | Traditional reading |
|---|---|---|
| 0° (same sign) | **Conjunction** | Most powerful angle — energies merge. **Ambivalent, not automatically "good"**: intensifies whatever is there; instant recognition, but shared flaws are shared too [1][9]. |
| 30° (adjacent sign) | **Semi-sextile** | Minor aspect; mild, internal friction between "next-door neighbours" — signs that share nothing elementally or in modality and have opposite polarity; requires small but constant adjustment [1]. |
| 60° | **Sextile** | Easy, friendly, cooperative — an opportunity aspect: harmonious but with more initiative required than the trine; can go unused if neither partner acts [1][9]. |
| 90° | **Square** | Hard — strong, friction-generating tension; forces decisions and action; classically the "challenge/growth" angle, highest conflict risk [1]. |
| 120° | **Trine** | Easy — "extremely beneficial" flow; effortless harmony; the caveat is that ease can be taken for granted [1]. |
| 150° | **Quincunx (inconjunct)** | Minor aspect — awkward, no common ground; the two signs share neither element, modality nor polarity; requires ongoing adjustment and is the classic "misfit" angle [1]. |
| 180° | **Opposition** | Hard-but-relational — pull between two poles; fundamentally about relationship and balance rather than unification; prone to exaggeration/pendulum swings; also the classic "opposites attract" angle [1][9]. |

The aspect between two signs is computed purely from their index difference around the wheel (see `pair-matrix.md` §2) — **VERIFIED** geometry: signs 1 apart are always semi-sextile, 2 apart sextile, 3 apart square, 4 apart trine, 5 apart quincunx, 6 apart opposition [1][2].

### 2.5 The role of ruling planets

Every sign has a planetary ruler [2][4]:

| Sign | Classical ruler | Modern co-ruler |
|---|---|---|
| Aries | Mars | — |
| Taurus | Venus | — |
| Gemini | Mercury | — |
| Cancer | Moon | — |
| Leo | Sun | — |
| Virgo | Mercury | — |
| Libra | Venus | — |
| Scorpio | Mars | Pluto |
| Sagittarius | Jupiter | — |
| Capricorn | Saturn | — |
| Aquarius | Saturn | Uranus |
| Pisces | Jupiter | Neptune |

(VERIFIED: table from [2]; the traditional/modern split rationale from [4]; modern assignments from [3].)

**How rulers feed compatibility readings:**

1. **Shared rulership = built-in affinity.** Gemini–Virgo share Mercury; Taurus–Libra share Venus. Traditionally, Scorpio–Aries shared Mars, Sagittarius–Pisces shared Jupiter and Capricorn–Aquarius shared Saturn before the outer planets were assigned; modern practice keeps those as "traditional rulers" with modern co-rulers. **DISPUTED:** how much affinity to credit the traditional-only shared pairs (Scorpio–Aries, Sagittarius–Pisces, Capricorn–Aquarius) in a modern-context product; our model credits the Mercury/Venus pairs more (see `pair-matrix.md` §3).
2. **Dignities of one sign's ruler inside the partner's sign.** Classical astrology assigns each planet a **detriment** (the sign opposite its domicile) and an **exaltation/fall** pair (VERIFIED table from [3]):

| Planet | Domicile | Detriment | Exalted in | Fall |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sun | Leo | Aquarius | Aries | Libra |
| Moon | Cancer | Capricorn | Taurus | Scorpio |
| Mercury | Gemini, Virgo | Sagittarius, Pisces | Virgo | Pisces |
| Venus | Taurus, Libra | Scorpio, Aries | Pisces | Virgo |
| Mars | Aries, Scorpio | Libra, Taurus | Capricorn | Cancer |
| Jupiter | Sagittarius, Pisces | Gemini, Virgo | Cancer | Capricorn |
| Saturn | Capricorn, Aquarius | Cancer, Leo | Libra | Aries |

   Reading: if sign A's ruler is dignified (especially exalted) inside sign B, A's energy is said to function well "in B's territory" — a supportive nuance (e.g., Leo's Sun is exalted in Aries → the Leo–Aries fire trine gets an extra classical blessing). If sign A's ruler is debilitated (detriment or fall) inside sign B, A's energy struggles there — a friction nuance (e.g., Cancer's Moon is in detriment in Capricorn → part of the Cancer–Capricorn axis tension).
3. **A structural consequence worth knowing (VERIFIED from the [3] table):** every classical ruler is debilitated exactly in its domicile's opposition, so **all six opposite-sign pairs have their rulers in mutual detriment**. This is a classical root of the "opposition = tension + magnetism" reading — it strengthens the case that the aspect lens should dominate over the element lens for opposition pairs (element says "complementary"; dignity + aspect say "polarised").
4. **Planet friendships/enemies (Vedic context only).** Vedic astrology ranks planet pairs as natural friends/neutrals/enemies (Graha Maitri, classical source Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra; web summaries [19][20 — UNVERIFIED URL]) — e.g., Sun–Moon/Mars/Jupiter friendships, Saturn's enmity with Sun/Moon/Mars, Venus–Saturn friendship, Mercury's enmity with Moon. Vyotish already uses Graha Maitri inside Ashtakoota matching. **Recommendation:** keep Graha Maitri OUT of the Western sun-sign scoring (it scores planets-in-signs across whole charts), but we may cross-link to Kundli matching for users who want the Vedic method. This keeps the two calculators methodologically honest rather than mixing systems.

### 2.6 How same-sign pairs are traditionally treated

**Not simply "perfect".** Findings:

- Cafe Astrology's aspect grid gives same-sign conjunction its **top** visual label ("Bang-On") and describes an "enormous and instant sense" of recognition between same-sign partners [9] — but the underlying aspect classification is **ambivalent**, not auto-positive: conjunction "intensifies whatever is there", so shared weaknesses amplify exactly as shared strengths do [1][9].
- Mainstream relationship writing on same-sign couples converges on the same nuance: instant understanding and strong chemistry on one side; mirrored bad habits, echo-chamber arguments and rivalry on the other; success depends on self-awareness and on "who bends" [22 — snippet, corroborated by [9]].
- Astrostyle treats same-sign pairs as ordinary pairings with their own pages, without declaring them best or worst [11].

**Conclusion for our product (adopted in `pair-matrix.md`):** same-sign pairs score in the **Strong band (80/100)**, below the top trines, with content that says: natural recognition and shared rhythm, but the relationship amplifies both partners' best and worst traits; growth requires one partner willing to move first. This is the defensible mainstream position, and it avoids the "same sign = soulmate" overclaim that several low-quality sites make (UNVERIFIED as to which sites do this; flagged as an observed pattern to avoid).

### 2.7 Synthesis: how the lenses combine (and where they disagree)

Because all four lenses are functions of wheel geometry, they agree far more than they disagree:

- trine = same element + complementary behaviour → both lenses say "easy";
- sextile = complementary elements + same polarity → both say "easy, with initiative";
- square = same modality + contrary/cross elements → both say "hard";
- semi-sextile/quincunx = no shared element, no shared modality, opposite polarity (adjacent) → both say "awkward";
- conjunction = everything shared → both say "intense, not auto-easy".

**The one systematic disagreement is the six opposition pairs** (Aries–Libra, Taurus–Scorpio, Gemini–Sagittarius, Cancer–Capricorn, Leo–Aquarius, Virgo–Pisces): the element lens says *complementary, supportive* elements; the aspect lens says *hard, polarising* angle, and the dignity lens adds *mutual detriment of rulers* ([1][3]). **Ruling: aspect dominates the band, element moderates.** Justification: classical sources classify the opposition among the "hard" aspects [1]; Cafe Astrology's grid gives oppositions their own category rather than filing them under easy [9]; and the mutual-detriment structure [3] reinforces the polarised reading. Our model therefore scores oppositions mid-band ("Mixed"), with the complementary-element bonus (+3) and content framing of "magnetic opposites with real work required". Full flag list and per-pair rulings: `pair-matrix.md` §6.

A second, smaller disagreement class: **easy aspects whose traditional dignities pull against them** (e.g., Cancer–Scorpio water trine, but Cancer's Moon is in fall in Scorpio and Scorpio's Mars in fall in Cancer [3]). Our model applies a bounded dignity modifier so the pair stays in the Strong band, and the content team gets the nuance as an explanation note rather than a score swing. Modern-ruler disputes (Scorpio/Aquarius/Pisces) are flagged per-pair.

## 3. How compatibility scores are conventionally presented

Observed conventions across the sites studied (all fetched directly unless noted; **no competitor score values were copied** — only formats):

| Format | Where observed | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Per-category percentages** | Astrotalk pair pages [8]; ZodiacSign.com pair pages [15]; mainstream syndicated content (Match.com) [18] | The dominant format on calculator-style sites. Each category shows a bare % (0–100). Astrotalk shows **no overall total** [8]; ZodiacSign.com adds a final "Summary" % [15]. |
| **Text verdicts, no numbers** | Astrostyle [11]; Astrology.com pair pages [13] | Editorial approach: prose only, or prose organised by method (Planets/Elements/Modalities). No score of any kind [11][13]. |
| **Aspect-class labels (7 classes, colour-coded)** | Cafe Astrology grid [9] | Maps each aspect to a label ("Bang-On" / "Hot" / "Harmonious" / "Opposites Attract" / "Huh?" / "Paradox" / "Clash" — labels named for structure only). Score-free but strictly deterministic. |
| **Overall single score** | ZodiacSign.com "Summary" % [15]; generic love-calculator sites (many observed in search results, not individually verified) | Common but not universal; Astrotalk notably omits it [8]. |
| **Star ratings** | **UNVERIFIED** on the major sites studied — not observed on Astrotalk, Astrostyle, Astrology.com, Cafe Astrology or ZodiacSign.com pair pages. Widely seen on small calculator sites (not verified individually). | Avoid as primary; optionally map our band to stars as decoration. |
| **Low / medium / high or "great / good / challenging" bands** | Observed as descriptive labels in text verdicts [11][18] and implied by Cafe Astrology's label classes [9] | Good fit for our "verification flags on verdicts" policy. |

**Recommendation (matches the owner's plan):** 0–100 overall score + band label (Excellent / Strong / Mixed / Challenging / Very challenging), plus one percentage per category, plus a short prose verdict. This combines the dominant calculator convention [8][15] with the editorial credibility of the no-score sites [11][13], and maps cleanly onto Vyotish's verification-flag pattern.

**Deterministic band derivation** is documented in `pair-matrix.md` (aspect base + element modifier + bounded dignity modifier → score → band), so the backend API can produce identical scores for any pair without human rating.

## 5. Content-safety guidance for the content team

These are requirements for whoever writes the pair/category copy later (fits Vyotish's existing no-fear, disclaimers-first policy):

1. **Framing:** every result page carries "for guidance and entertainment purposes only" near the score AND in the footer (Astrotalk only footers it [8]; we should do both — differentiator, and honest).
2. **No absolute claims:** never "you will", "never work", "guaranteed", "destined", "soulmates — proven". Use "may", "often", "tends to", "a common reading is".
3. **No medical, mental-health, pregnancy/fertility, legal or financial claims** in any category copy (e.g., no "Sexual compatibility issues your health"; no "Marriage compatibility proves you should marry"). Money/work themes live only in generic tips ("talk about budgets") without astrological causation.
4. **No fear-based copy:** low scores are "challenging / needs work / growth-oriented", never "toxic", "doomed", "dangerous". Astrostyle's stance (no pair is declared incompatible; difficulty is framed as growth) is a good editorial north star [11].
5. **Verification flags:** every astrological verdict displayed on Vyotish carries the site's standard verification flag (method used: aspect + element + ruler consensus; not a scientific measure). Cite the method, not a person.
6. **Gender & orientation neutrality:** cards must read identically for any couple; avoid "man/woman" copy patterns (GaneshaSpeaks' gendered input is a pattern to avoid [14]).
7. **Age/stage sensitivity:** avoid advice implying relationship stage (marriage pressure, "too young"), following Astrostyle's restraint [11].
8. **QA lesson from observation:** Astrotalk's pair page showed signs of templating errors (supporting paragraphs referencing the wrong signs) [8]. Our backend must bind every sentence to the actual pair ID; add an automated QA check that each rendered page mentions exactly the two selected signs.
